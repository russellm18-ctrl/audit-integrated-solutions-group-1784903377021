## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your hero image is a wide panoramic shot of Mount Baker at dusk — snow-capped peak, warm sunset gradient, no people, no work, no client relationship visible anywhere in the frame. It is geographically beautiful and professionally irrelevant. Your copy directly beneath it reads: "We want to be your consultant of choice for the benefit of the communities you serve." That is a promise about human partnership and public-sector impact. The image delivers a screensaver. The emotional gap between those two signals — aspirational landscape versus people-centered consulting — is the first thing a procurement director or agency CIO registers, even if they cannot name it. They clock "stock photo energy" and discount your credibility before reading a single service line.

The Great Place To Work badge and the "All Staff.jpg" photograph exist on the same homepage, but the staff photo is cropped, buried below the fold, and sandwiched between award announcements. The humans who are supposedly your differentiator — "smart, diligent and dedicated individuals" — appear as an afterthought to a mountain. That sequencing tells buyers your geography matters more than your team. The trust cost: a high-value public agency comparing vendors will weight the firm whose people appear confident and specific over the firm whose hero communicates "we are located near pretty things."

## Visual Narrative Coherence

Your five internal anchor links — `#comp-m22hr3gs2` through `#comp-m22htmi3` — render as bare hash fragments with zero descriptive text in the scan output. To a buyer clicking through your service sections, these are invisible navigation ghosts. No broken links were detected across the nine homepage links audited, so the underlying structure holds, but the anchor labeling fracture signals that your site was assembled rather than designed for a buyer journey. A $300 million SI contract manager comparing you to a polished competitor will not chase unlabeled anchors — they will bounce to whoever makes the next step obvious.

The award assets compound this. "Government Business Review Consultancy of the Year" and "Top 10 Most Promising Public Sector Services Companies of 2023" are genuinely strong proof points. Both are presented as static image badges with filenames `image.png` and `Untitled%20design%20(1).png` — zero context, zero story, zero visual connection to the work that earned them. Buyers who cannot link an award to a recognizable project moment treat it as decoration, not evidence.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your page title is "isg-nw| Olympia Wa" — 18 characters, no service category, no value statement. Every competitor bidding on public-sector consulting terms in Washington State is writing titles that include the words buyers actually search. You rank #1 for your own brand name, which means people who already know you can find you. People who do not know you — the new procurement officers, the agency IT directors searching "organizational change management consultant Washington State" — encounter a title that tells them nothing and a meta description that has to carry the entire positioning load alone.

One of your eight content images is missing alt text entirely (13% coverage gap). The image with the filename `Untitled%20design%20(1).png` — your "Top 10 Most Promising" award graphic — is the most likely culprit given that naming pattern. That filename is also what search engines index as image content. "Untitled design" returns zero relevance signal for any public-sector keyword you want to own. The `og:image` tag is present, which preserves your LinkedIn share appearance, but the title weakness means every organic social click lands on a page that undersells a firm managing $300M in contracts.
